# The relief ratio collapses under ambient light, and the strobe difference restores it

Measured 2026-08-04, same camera and subject as
[the relief report](2026-08-04-landmark-relief.md), with the subject at a window
in afternoon daylight falling on the left side of the face, glasses on. Corpus:
`2026-08-04-ambient.jsonl`, 40 frames, every one carrying a detected mesh, each
labelled with its frame mean and emitter phase.

This began as an attempt at the strobe differential, the idea that an occluder's
darkness sits in the material while a geometric shadow's sits in the emitter
geometry, so the emitter-off frame should tell them apart. That question is only
half answered here, because the print could not be brought to the window. What
the capture settled instead is a property of the cue itself.

## Validity first

The first attempt at this capture is discarded. The subject sat close enough
that the chin landmarks fell at y = 398 to 413 in a 400-row frame, so the
sampler clamped at the border and returned 0 for every fully out-of-frame
landmark. The two strobe phases then disagreed for a purely geometric reason:
with the emitter on, all five chin points sat outside the frame, and with it off
they sat two pixels higher and caught the last row.

The relief report lists chin visibility as a precondition. This is what
violating it looks like, and it produces confident-looking numbers rather than
an error. Every frame in the corpus below has all five chin landmarks strictly
inside the frame.

## Ambient light degrades the raw cue

Region medians, and the ratios formed from them:

| frames | cheek | chin | forehead | cheek/chin | chin/forehead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| emitter on (emitter + daylight) | 107.7 | 55.4 | 114.4 | 1.911 | 0.494 |
| emitter off (daylight only) | 57.5 | 39.3 | 37.7 | 1.440 | 1.054 |
| emitter only (on minus off) | 50.4 | 16.2 | 76.7 | 3.056 | 0.213 |

The same subject measured in a dim room reads cheek/chin 2.580-3.532 and
chin/forehead 0.192. At the window the **raw** emitter-on ratio reads 1.911, and
the full range across the twenty lit frames is 1.879-2.134.

That is below the face band the relief report derived, and close to the print
population it was supposed to separate from, which spans 1.295-1.610. A band
gate calibrated in a dim room would reject this genuine user for sitting near a
window. The relief cue as measured is not a property of the face alone; it moves
with the light in the room, which is the failure #174 describes for the existing
absolute thresholds, reproduced in the candidate meant to replace them.

## The strobe difference removes it

Subtracting the adjacent emitter-off frame leaves only the emitter's own
contribution. Over 20 lit/ambient pairs, none dropped, the emitter contributed a
median 50.4 at the cheek, 16.2 at the chin and 76.7 at the forehead, and the
resulting ratio is:

- emitter-only cheek/chin **3.056**, range 2.808-3.364
- emitter-only chin/forehead **0.213**

Both land inside the dim-room figures (2.580-3.532 and 0.192) that the same
subject produced with no meaningful ambient at all. The differential is
therefore the quantity that should be gated, not the raw lit frame: it is what
the cue was always trying to measure, and it survives a change in room lighting
that moves the raw value by a third.

## The occluder discriminator: measured, and refuted

With daylight arriving from the left, the emitter-off chin reads 1.054 of the
forehead, so the chin shadow disappears when the emitter does. A black cotton
sock wrapped over the chin, captured facing the window, reads 0.158 in the same
phase: dark with the emitter off, as an absorbing material should be. Side by
side those two numbers look like a discriminator.

They are not. The two captures had different lighting, so a control was taken:
the same subject, sock removed, same position facing the sun, same distance.
Corpus `2026-08-04-sun-control.jsonl`.

| condition | emitter on | emitter off | off / on |
|---|---|---|---|
| bare face, sun on face | 0.309 | 0.210 | 0.68x |
| sock over chin, sun on face | 0.221 | 0.158 | 0.71x |

A bare chin and an occluded one behave the same. **The emitter-off frame does
not distinguish them.** The 1.054 reading came from the light arriving at the
side of the face, where it reaches the chin; with the sun frontal and above, the
jaw shadows the chin from ambient much as it does from the coaxial emitter, and
a genuine chin stays dark with the emitter off.

The ambient direction therefore decides the answer, and the attacker does not
control it but neither does the gate. This is the failure mode the limits
section of an earlier revision predicted; it is now measured rather than
predicted, and it removes the last candidate defence this work had against an
absorbing occluder.

Had the control been skipped, the two figures from different sessions would have
been published as a working discriminator.

## The subject has a beard, and it was never recorded

Rendering the infrared frames and looking at them, which should have happened
before any of this analysis, shows the sole subject has a full beard covering
the chin and jaw. Every "chin" reading in this corpus and in the relief report
is therefore a reading of beard hair, not of skin.

That matters because real hair absorbs 850nm while printed hair does not, which
this project already measured on a smaller region: a real eyebrow reads 0.728 of
forehead against a printed eyebrow's 0.961. The chin is the same comparison at
larger scale, with the real beard at 0.192 of forehead and the printed one at
0.625. So the cue may be separating real hair from printed hair rather than
relief from flatness, and those two explanations predict different behaviour on
a clean-shaven face: one keeps working, the other does not.

This corpus cannot separate them. The emitter-off frame argues against pure
absorption, since the chin reads 1.054 of the forehead there rather than staying
dark, but both regions are dimly lit at that point, 39.3 and 37.7, because the
daylight arrives from the side and mostly lights the background. The honest
position is that geometry and facial hair are entangled in every chin
measurement taken so far.

The relief report lists a beard among the things that would change what is
sampled. It is not a hypothetical: it is a property of the only subject
measured, and the report's single-subject caveat is correspondingly more serious
than it reads.

## Limits

One subject, one session, one window, glasses on throughout. Daylight arrived
from one side; overhead or backlit ambient would shadow the chin differently and
could make the emitter-off chin dark for a genuine user, which is the failure
mode this discriminator would have. The emitter-only subtraction assumes the
scene is unchanged across the 71 ms between paired frames, which a seated
subject satisfies and a moving one may not.
